Connect in four steps

You need two things: a free Intervals.icu account, and Claude or ChatGPT. The coach reads the training data already in that account. No account yet? Step three takes you to the Intervals.icu sign-in page, where Google gets you one in about 30 seconds.

Connection address (remote MCP URL) https://mcp.paceandstaystrong.com/mcp
  1. Open your AI's connector settings.

    Look for the option to add a connector.

    claude.ai or Claude Desktop: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector.

    ChatGPT: full read-and-write access is in beta on Business, Enterprise, and Edu web workspaces, enabled through Apps / developer mode. Otherwise, wait for this connector to appear in the ChatGPT directory.

  2. Paste the connection address.

    Put the address above in the remote MCP server URL field and continue. MCP is the standard interface an AI uses to reach an outside service — pasting the address is all it asks of you.

  3. Approve access in Intervals.icu.

    When Intervals.icu shows its consent screen, sign in, check all four permissions, and approve. Each box covers a different kind of access. Skip one, and the feature that needs it breaks in a way that's hard to trace back — check all four for the full coaching flow.

    What that grant covers, and how to revoke it.

  4. Go back to your AI and start talking.

    Make sure the connector is switched on in the conversation, then ask the coach to read your recent training.

Try this first: “Read my recent training and set a 28-day direction for running and strength.”

Is your Intervals.icu account new or empty?

The coach reads whatever is already in Intervals.icu. If the account is new, there are two ways to bring your history in before you ask it to read your training:

  • Connect a device or app you already use. In Intervals.icu's own Settings, connect Garmin or whatever watch or training app you have. Past activity shows up automatically — once it lands, ask the coach to read your training again.
  • Hand the coach an export. Send it a CSV, an Apple Health export, or a .fit file in the conversation and ask it to import your history. (The file goes to the coach, not into Intervals.icu.)

One current plan for running and strength.

Life changes, so training should too. A missed run, a lifting session you actually finished, recovery data that's incomplete — all of that should shape what's next. Long Run Hybrid Coach checks the plan against what you actually did, and updates your one current direction.

1

One current direction

The next 28 days and the current week change together. There is no folder of stale plans to reconcile by hand.

2

Run and lift together

Running and strength share the same week and recovery budget. Strength is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

3

Adapts to what really happened

It looks at what you actually did, and whatever recovery data is available, before changing anything. If something's missing or out of date, the plan treats it as unknown — not as zero.

Strength stays specific.

“Back squat, 4×5 at 100 kg” is a real session in your plan, not a note you left yourself. Intervals.icu receives the session title; the movement, sets, reps and load stay in the conversation, where you can read them back any time.

How it works

Your devices and apps send data to Intervals.icu — how much detail you get depends on the device, but the coaching itself works the same either way. The coach reads that training data, plus anything you tell it directly.

Before writing You see the full workout and approve it.

Calendar permission means the coach can propose a workout — it can't write anything until you confirm the full preview you saw.

After writing The coach checks back with Intervals.icu.

A successful delivery means Intervals.icu saved the workout and the coach read it back to confirm. It doesn't mean your watch has it yet.

Questions before you connect

Is this an app I install?

No. It runs inside a conversation with Claude or ChatGPT — you connect it once, then you just talk to it. There is nothing to download, no separate account to create here, and no dashboard to keep checking. Your plan lives in the coach and your workouts land in your Intervals.icu calendar.

What do I need to start?

An Intervals.icu account, and Claude or ChatGPT. The four steps above have the address to paste in. If you don't have an Intervals.icu account yet, it's free — the setup flow takes you to its sign-in page, where you can create one with Google in about 30 seconds. Claude works on its free plan. ChatGPT currently needs a Business, Enterprise, or Edu workspace — on a personal plan, wait for this coach to appear in the ChatGPT directory.

Do I need a particular watch?

No. The coach works from whatever completed activities and wellness data end up in your Intervals.icu account.

Can I run my own gateway instead of the hosted one?

Yes. Self-hosting runs the exact same product with your own Intervals.icu OAuth credentials and your own storage — it's for developers who want to run it themselves. Most people should just use the hosted address in the setup steps.

Why do all four Intervals.icu permissions need to be checked?

The four permissions let the coach read your completed activities, read your wellness data, read and write your training calendar, and read your Run settings. Your Run settings hold your threshold pace — if it's missing, the coach can only fill it in through a workout you've already confirmed. Each box covers a different kind of access. Skip one, and the feature that needs it breaks in a way that's hard to trace back — check all four for the full coaching flow.

Can the coach write a workout without my approval?

No. Every workout is shown to you in full first, and it's only written after you confirm exactly what you saw.

What does a successful delivery mean?

A successful delivery means Intervals.icu saved the workout and the coach read it back to confirm. It doesn't mean your watch has it yet.

How do I revoke access or delete my data?

Want your stored data deleted too? Ask the coach in the conversation first — it shows you exactly what's being removed before you confirm. Then remove this application in your Intervals.icu settings to stop future reads and writes. (If you revoke first, the coach is signed out and can no longer delete data for you in the conversation.) Because Intervals.icu grants access per application, not per AI, removing it disconnects every AI you'd connected — Claude, ChatGPT, all of them. Workouts already on your Intervals.icu calendar stay yours to keep or remove. More detail on the support page.
